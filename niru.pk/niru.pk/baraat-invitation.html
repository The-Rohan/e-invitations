Baraat Invitation Cards: Getting the Arrival Right

What is the Baraat, and what does its invitation need to carry?

The Baraat is the groom's procession: his family and friends, often with a dhol, arriving at the wedding venue to bring him to his bride. On most Pakistani cards "Baraat & Nikah" appear together because they usually happen on the same day, often the same evening. The Baraat arrives, there is an entrance, the Nikah is read, dinner and photographs follow, and the Rukhsati closes the night. The Walima is often the next day.

This is the function where timing matters most. Guests need to know when the Baraat is expected to arrive, not just when the hall opens: too early and they wait around, too late and they miss the entrance everyone came for. The invitation should also be precise about the venue, which is often different from where the Mehndi was held, and give a dress code a notch more formal than the Mehndi: rich jewel tones and formal eastern wear.

Hosting on the Baraat day traditionally sits with the bride's family, but this varies by family and region, so name whichever families are actually hosting rather than assume.

Wording notes

Baraat wording sits between the Mehndi's playfulness and the Nikah's formality: warm but composed, naming both families, and clear about arrival time and ceremony time if they differ. Write the groom's name first and the bride's second, as Pakistani stationery always has.

Questions people ask

Should the Baraat and Nikah be one section or two?

Either, depending on your day. If both happen at the same venue back to back, one combined section with an arrival time and a ceremony time works well. If they are at different venues or times, give each its own.

What time should I put on a Baraat card?

The time the procession is actually expected to arrive, not the hall's opening time. That is what guests plan around, and it stops people missing the entrance.

Who traditionally hosts the Baraat?

Most commonly the bride's family, though it varies by family and region. The invitation should simply name whichever families are hosting.

Can the Baraat dress code differ from the Mehndi's on the same invitation?

Yes. Each function carries its own dress-code line, so the Mehndi's bright colours and the Baraat's formal jewel tones sit on the same invitation without confusing anyone.

Does the Baraat need its own RSVP?

Guests choose which functions they are attending, so your private response sheet shows the Baraat headcount separately from the Walima's, which is what the caterer needs.

Give your Baraat its own time, its own map and its own RSVP.
