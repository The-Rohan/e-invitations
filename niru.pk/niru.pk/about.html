About Niru

A Lahore studio, built around one idea

Niru is a small studio in Lahore that makes digital wedding invitations for Pakistani weddings, founded by Hanzla Nadeem. The idea behind it is simple: a wedding invitation should feel as considered as a well-made printed card, and it should also work the way people actually send invitations now, which is a link on WhatsApp. It is a small studio by choice. Every card that goes out has been looked at by a person before a guest sees it.

Why digital, for a Pakistani wedding

Printed cards still matter to many families, and Niru isn't arguing against them. But most guest lists are reached on WhatsApp regardless, usually through a photo of the printed card forwarded around family groups, too small to read and impossible to update. Niru is built for that gap: an invitation made to be sent digitally in the first place, with the care that goes into a printed one (proper typography, a considered entrance, a design that holds up on a phone) plus what paper can't do: an RSVP that counts itself, maps, and a link that updates if something changes.

How we work

Every Niru card starts from one of the studio's live designs or, for a Personalised Edition, from a brief about your wedding. Your details come in, and the card is built and finished by hand: the wording, the photos, the Urdu, the functions. You see a full preview before anything goes to a single guest, and nothing reaches your guest list without your approval.

Depending on the tier, the first preview arrives 24 hours to five days after your details and payment are confirmed. Revisions continue from there: up to five rounds on Signature templates, unlimited within the approved direction on Premium, Atelier and every Personalised Edition. Once delivered, the invitation stays live for a year, and longer on request.

Bilingual by default

Every card carries English and Urdu in the same invitation, switched with one tap, because that is how most Pakistani guest lists actually read. The Urdu is written as Urdu rather than translated line by line, and set in Nastaliq. Read more about Urdu wedding invitations.

What "premium" means here

It is an easy word to overuse, so it is worth being specific. On Niru, every design has its own entrance and its own art direction rather than being a recoloured copy of another, the Urdu is composed rather than translated, and every card is checked on a phone before it is sent. It shows up in small decisions, such as how an entrance opens, where a Bismillah sits, and how a line of Nastaliq is sized against the English beside it, more than in any single feature.

Our cultural commitments

Niru's designs stay within Pakistani wedding convention. Mehndi, Baraat, Nikah and Walima are written in their own terms; host families are named alongside the couple; the groom's name comes first and the bride's second; and there is no alcohol-related content or out-of-place Western imagery anywhere in the collection. Where a card carries the Bismillah or a Quranic verse, it keeps its place in the design. Read about our Islamic wedding invitations.

Questions people ask

Where is Niru based?

Niru is a studio in Lahore, Pakistan. Every invitation is delivered as a link, so couples order from anywhere in Pakistan and abroad.

Who is behind Niru?

Niru was founded by Hanzla Nadeem. The studio handles the design, the build and the finishing of every invitation.

Do you make printed cards too?

No. Niru makes digital invitations only: a link guests open on their phone, with no app or login.

Does someone look at my card before it goes to guests?

Yes. Every invitation is finished by hand and previewed for your approval before it goes live, in every tier.

How do I contact the studio?

WhatsApp 0321 3397889 or email support@niru.pk, 10:00 am to midnight, Monday to Saturday. The studio is closed on Sunday.

Can guests abroad open the invitation?

Yes. It is a link that opens in any phone's browser, so it works the same for a guest in Lahore, Dubai or London.
