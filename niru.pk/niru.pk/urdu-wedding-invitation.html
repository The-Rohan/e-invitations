Urdu Wedding Invitations, Written as Urdu and Not Translated

What is an Urdu wedding invitation on Niru?

Every Niru invitation carries English and Urdu in the same card, and each guest switches between them with one tap: no second link and no second design. For many Pakistani families that one tap solves a real problem. The grandparent who wants to read the invitation in Urdu and the cousin abroad who is more comfortable in English open exactly the same card.

The Urdu is set in Nastaliq, the calligraphic script Urdu is actually written in, not a flat Naskh substitute that looks like Arabic on a website. And the Urdu text is composed as Urdu, not translated line by line from the English draft, so it reads the way a family would write it.

Families who read Urdu, and families who read English

On most guest lists this is not a clean split by generation; it is mixed within the same household. A father may prefer Urdu and his children English; an uncle abroad may only read English. A printed card forced a choice: one language, on paper, for everyone. A digital invitation doesn't have to. The switch lives on the card, so you don't decide in advance who gets which version. Every guest decides for themselves.

Nastaliq, not just "Urdu script"

Type matters more on a wedding card than almost anywhere else: it is the first thing every guest reads, on a phone, at a glance. Nastaliq is harder to set well on screen than most scripts. It needs generous line height, room for its diagonal flow, and a size that doesn't clip its loops. Niru's cards are built with that in mind, so the Urdu side is designed with the same care as the English rather than squeezed in beside it.

One tap between English and Urdu

The language switch sits on the card itself. Open the invitation, tap once, and the whole card changes language: the headings, the function names, the dates, the RSVP form. It is a small mechanism, but it is the difference between an invitation that serves one side of the family and one that serves everyone on the list.

Getting the wording right

Pakistani invitations carry more structure than a typical English card: the host families are named, the guest's presence is requested formally, the couple is named with their parentage, and the function names (Mehndi, Baraat, Nikah, Walima, Mayun or a Qawwali night) stay in their own words rather than being translated into something generic. Niru writes both sides to match. The English carries the formality Pakistani stationery expects, and the Urdu is written the way the sentiment is actually spoken, including the few English words people naturally use in Urdu, rather than mirroring the English sentence by sentence.

If you are drafting your own wording before you order, the wedding invitation wording guide has examples for every function in both languages.

Urdu

دونوں خاندانوں کی دعاؤں کے ساتھابراہیم اور حانیہاپنے نکاح اور ولیمے کی تقریبات میںآپ کی شرکت کے منتظر ہیں۔

The Urdu is not a word-for-word rendering of the English. "Request the honour of your presence" becomes "آپ کی شرکت کے منتظر ہیں", which is how the sentiment is naturally written in Urdu.

Questions people ask

Is the Urdu on Niru cards machine-translated?

No. The Urdu is written as Urdu by the studio and checked against the English for meaning, then set in Nastaliq. You see it in your preview before anything is sent to guests.

Can guests switch language themselves, or do I send two links?

One link. The language switch is on the invitation itself, so every guest opens the same card and taps to read it in English or Urdu.

Do guests need to install an Urdu font?

No. The invitation opens in the phone's normal browser with nothing to install. Open any live demo on your own phone and switch to Urdu to see exactly how it looks.

Can I see and change the Urdu wording before the card is sent?

Yes. You receive a full preview in both languages before the invitation goes live. Signature templates include up to five revision rounds; Premium, Atelier and every Personalised Edition include unlimited revisions within the approved direction.

Do I need to provide the Urdu myself?

No. Give us your details in English or Urdu, whichever is easier, and we write both versions. If you already have family wording you want used exactly, send it and we will set it as it is.
