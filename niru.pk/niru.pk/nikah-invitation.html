Nikah Card Design: Getting the Solemn Part Right

What is the Nikah, and what should its invitation carry?

The Nikah is the marriage contract itself: the religious ceremony, read by a Nikah Khwan in front of witnesses, with the bride's consent, her wali and the agreed mehr. It is the one function of a Pakistani wedding that is solemn rather than celebratory. Some families hold it as a small, separate gathering, close family only, sometimes in the daytime and sometimes weeks before the Baraat and Walima. Others hold it on the Baraat evening itself.

Either way, a Nikah invitation sits in a different register from everything else on the wedding: a modest dress code, seating that may be separate for ladies and gentlemen, and wording that is formal even when the gathering is small. Many families open it with the Bismillah and a Quranic verse; Ar-Rum 30:21, on tranquillity, love and mercy between spouses, is the one most Pakistani wedding cards carry.

Wording notes

Open with the Bismillah if your family wants it, name the host families, then the couple with their parentage, groom first. Keep the tone measured: this is the place for "with the blessings of Allah and our families" rather than "let's celebrate".

How Niru handles a Nikah invitation

A Niru invitation carries what a Nikah needs: an optional Bismillah banner and the Ar-Rum 30:21 verse in Arabic with English and Urdu meaning, an optional note that seating is separate for ladies and gentlemen, the Nikah's own venue and time distinct from the Baraat's, and an optional note that the Nikah will be registered.

Nikah-only gatherings are often smaller than the rest of the wedding, so they are also the function most likely to need their own guest list: a separate link (PKR 1,500) for the family invited to the Nikah itself, apart from the wider Baraat and Walima list.

The whole card switches between English and Urdu with one tap, with Urdu set in Nastaliq. That matters most here, because the Nikah is usually where the Urdu wording has to feel exactly right for the elders reading it.

Questions people ask

Can I include the Bismillah and a Quranic verse on my Nikah invitation?

Yes. Both are optional and chosen when you order: the Bismillah banner, and Ar-Rum 30:21 in Arabic with its meaning in English and Urdu. You can also ask for a different verse or dua.

How do I tell guests that seating is segregated?

Choose the seating note when you order. It appears with the venue details, so guests know before they arrive rather than finding out at the door.

Should the Nikah have a separate guest list from the Baraat and Walima?

Often, yes, because Nikah gatherings are usually smaller. A Nikah-only link (PKR 1,500) lets you invite the wider circle to the Baraat and Walima without extending the same list to the Nikah.

Is Urdu important on a Nikah card?

It is often where it matters most, because religious wording tends to read more naturally in Urdu for elders. Niru writes the Urdu as Urdu, set in Nastaliq, and guests switch languages with one tap.

Can the Nikah date be different from the Baraat date on the same invitation?

Yes. Each function has its own date, venue and time, whether the Nikah is weeks before the Baraat or on the same evening.
