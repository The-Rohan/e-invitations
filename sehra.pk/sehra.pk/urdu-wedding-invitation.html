Urdu wedding invitations, written the way you would say it

A wedding invitation should sound like the family it comes from. Our cards show Urdu in proper Nastaliq next to English, and guests choose their language with one tap.

Two languages, one card

Every name, heading, event and line of wording can be written in English and in Urdu. Guests see the language they prefer, and can switch at any time. Elders read the Urdu; cousins abroad read the English; both open the same link.

Nastaliq, not a font substitute

Urdu is shown in a Nastaliq typeface with the spacing it needs, right to left, so names and verses look the way they do on a printed card rather than like a screenshot of a form.

You do not need to type Urdu to start

If your family writes in Urdu, you can paste it in. If not, send us the names and we can help with the standard wording. Either way, you see the result on your phone before anything is final.

Questions people ask

Can the whole card be in Urdu?

Yes. You can write everything in Urdu, in English, or in both, and choose which language the card opens in.

Does the Urdu look right on every phone?

Yes. The Nastaliq font is loaded with the card, so it does not depend on what the guest has installed.
