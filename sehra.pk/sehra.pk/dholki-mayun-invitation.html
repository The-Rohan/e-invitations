Dholki and Mayun invitations for the nights before the wedding

Dholki and Mayun are the gatherings where the wedding really begins: songs at home, haldi, ubtan and blessings. They deserve an invitation that feels as warm and informal as the evening.

Many evenings, many addresses

A Dholki is rarely one night. It moves from house to house, a different aunt hosting each evening, and the Mayun happens at the bride’s home and then the groom’s. A single card with a list of dates and addresses gets confusing quickly.

Give each gathering its own entry with its own date, time, address and map, or give the whole family one card that lists them all in order. Guests tap the evening they were invited to and the directions open on their phone.

Keep it intimate

  • Informal, warm wording in English, Urdu or both, with a line for family only or close friends only.
  • A private link: it is not listed on Google and only people you send it to can open it.
  • A guest list per evening, so a small Dholki stays small.
  • RSVP with a headcount, so the host at home knows how many chairs to put out.

Wording you can borrow

An evening of songs and laughter.
Come sing with us at the Dholki, Tuesday 1 December, 7 pm, at our home. Bring a song.

Questions people ask

Can one card list several Dholki nights?

Yes. Add each evening as its own event with its own date, time and address, or make a separate card for each one if different people are invited to each.

Can I keep the Mayun for family only?

Yes. Send the Mayun card only to the people you want. Cards are private links, so nobody else sees them.
