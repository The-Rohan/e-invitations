WhatsApp Invitation Etiquette for Pakistani Weddings

How to send your invitation without landing in the wrong group, annoying your khala, or getting lost in a chat with 340 unread messages.

ZR

Zainab Rehman

Community & Culture

پیغام

WhatsApp is where your invitation will live or die. It is also where your invitation can accidentally reach four hundred people you did not intend to invite. A little discipline here saves a lot of awkwardness later.

Never send it to a group first

Groups are ambiguous. A wedding invitation posted in a fifty-person family group invites all fifty, plus anyone they mention it to. If your guest list is smaller than the group, you have just created a problem you cannot politely undo.

The rule

Send individually or to a broadcast list you have curated. Use groups only when the group genuinely maps to your guest list — for example, a group made specifically for the wedding.

Write a message that stands on its own

A bare link looks like spam and gets ignored. Two lines of warmth make it an invitation. Address the person by name, mention the relationship, then the link.

Assalam-o-Alaikum Khala jaan! Ammar and Bint are getting married, and we would be so happy to have you with us. All the details, timings and the map are here — and you can let us know if you'll be joining right from the page.

Send at the right time of day

  • Best: 11am–1pm, or 7pm–9pm. People are awake, unhurried, and on their phones.
  • Avoid: after 10pm. Elders will see a late-night notification and worry something is wrong.
  • Avoid: Friday during Jummah.

Send in waves, not all at once

If you send to three hundred contacts in ten minutes, WhatsApp may flag the account, and you will spend the evening explaining to your fiancé why your number is restricted. Spread the send over two or three days, and start with the people whose reply you most want.

Handle the reply flood

You will get a hundred 'Mubarak ho!' messages and no useful information. That is exactly why the RSVP lives on the invitation page and not in your chats. When someone replies with congratulations, thank them warmly and add: 'Do tap the RSVP on the page so we can plan the seating.'

Preview before you send

WhatsApp generates a preview card from the link. Open your published invitation once in a browser before the big send, so the preview image and title are cached and every guest sees the card rather than a naked URL.
